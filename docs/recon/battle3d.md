# Recon: Battle three.js scene — mat, backdrop, lighting, materials, camera, post, leaders (src/ui/battle/scene.ts, board.ts, cardMesh.ts, leaderMesh.ts, src/ui/art/boards.ts)

**Score: 4/10** — 16 defects (4 critical)

## The single worst thing

The mat is a hard-edged opaque rectangle that is, provably, uniformly lit — and those are the same defect. board.ts:131 builds it as `BoxGeometry(21, 0.42, 17.6)` with an opaque MeshStandardMaterial: four 90-degree corners, no alphaMap, no bevel, no feather. Under an orthographic camera an axis-aligned box projects to an axis-aligned screen rectangle, so it can never be anything but a panel. Then scene.ts:291-307 lights it with `AmbientLight(0x8878c8, 1.5)` plus a `DirectionalLight(1.9)`; on a flat y=0 plane both terms are mathematically constant across all 370 world units of surface, so there is literally zero lighting gradient on the mat — every bit of "shading" you see is the baked canvas gradient from board.ts:35. The only spatial variation comes from two point lights (scene.ts:310-316) that have no emitter object, and they land as two soft round dabs at roughly (500,250) and (1100,690) at 1600x900, reading as Photoshop soft-brush blobs. The result in canvas-only.png is unambiguous: a violet slab with a wallpaper hex tile, hard-cut into a painted rooftop, with a neon 1px rule drawn around the outside of it. It is a `<div>` with `border: 1px solid` on a background-image.

## Defects

### 1. [CRITICAL] The board mat is an opaque hard-edged rectangle with 90-degree corners, punched into the painted backdrop with no transition of any kind. In crop-mat-corner.png (1:1 pixels) the boundary between the painted city and the mat is a literal 1px step: blue #1b3a6b to #0e1030 with nothing between them. No feather, no light spill, no dust, no contact. The mat is DARKER than the sky behind it at the top edge, so it reads as a hole punched in the picture rather than ground lying in it.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:131-142 (BoxGeometry(BOARD.width, 0.42, BOARD.depth), opaque MeshStandardMaterial, no alphaMap); visible at x 310-1288, y 45-858 in scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/canvas-only.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §2 names this exact failure by name: "the battle backdrop art is beautiful and is currently ~80% covered by an opaque mat with a hard rectangular edge. A mat must feel like ground inside the place, not a panel dropped on a photo. Bleed it, light it, let the environment reach onto it." §1 also bans "a solid #hex on any surface larger than an icon" and demands an edge treatment. Hearthstone's board has no straight edge anywhere — the play surface is an irregular painted island inside a carved wooden rim whose props (tavern candles, Un'Goro ferns) physically overlap the play area. Gwent's ground is a chamfered lozenge that dissolves into scenery on all four sides.
- **Fix:** Delete the BoxGeometry. Replace with a `PlaneGeometry(BOARD.width * 1.25, BOARD.depth * 1.25, 64, 64)` laid at y=0 carrying a canvas-generated alphaMap: a superellipse (exponent ~4) with a ~2.5-unit corner radius and a ~1.8-unit feather on every edge, so the silhouette has no straight run and no corner. Author it as a shared `makeSoftMaskTexture()` primitive so the leader pools, the drop zones and the DOM panels feather on the same curve. Delete the four `edgeAlong`/`edgeSide` rails (board.ts:146-166) in the same change — they are what draws the rectangle.

### 2. [CRITICAL] The mat receives no lighting gradient at all. Ambient (intensity 1.5, saturated 0x8878c8) plus a directional (1.9) on a flat horizontal plane are both spatially constant — N·L for the directional is 0.84 everywhere, so its contribution is a fixed 1.60 across the entire surface. The intense saturated ambient is the single largest light term and ambient has no direction by definition. The only variation is from two point lights that produce isolated round hotspots with no visible source object.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:291-322 (ambient 1.5, key 1.9, two PointLights at 34/30, SpotLight 42 with decay 1.6 at 13 units contributing ~0.75)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1: "A gradient with a light source. One consistent key light per screen. If the top edge of one panel is lit and the top edge of the next is not, the screen has two suns." This screen has four: the key says upper-left, the cyan hotspot says upper-left-far, the magenta hotspot says lower-right-near, and the baked canvas radial says centre-out. None agree. In Hearthstone every light on the board has a visible emitter you can point at — the braziers, the tavern hearth, the Un'Goro lava — and the board's brightness falls off measurably toward the corners.
- **Fix:** Cut `ambient` to ~0.35 and desaturate it toward 0xa89ec8. Build a `scene.environment` from a PMREMGenerator over a canvas-drawn gradient cubemap (magenta from the player side, cyan from the enemy side, warm city bounce at the horizon) — PMREMGenerator ships inside `three`, so this is £0 and no new dependency. Bake a directional light/AO gradient into `makeTableTexture()` that matches the key at (-6,14,7) rather than the current centre-out radial. Give `rimWarm`/`rimCool` visible emitter objects (a small glowing practical sprite at each position) so the hotspots have a cause.

### 3. [CRITICAL] The enemy leader renders the missing-art placeholder: a rust-coloured scalloped disc with a black keyhole glyph where a face should be. The player's leader in the same frame has a real portrait. So the two leaders are visibly different classes of object, and the one you are trying to kill is the broken one.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/leaderMesh.ts:71-79 feeding src/ui/cardRenderer/renderLeader.ts (drawPlaceholderArt fallback); visible at (800,160) in scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/board.png and 1:1 in crop-leader.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §0: put this beside a Hearthstone screenshot and the missing-image icon on the enemy hero is the first thing anyone points at. Hearthstone's hero portrait is a painted bust in a carved gold-and-stone frame with a real bevel, seated in a recessed tray with a shadow, an armour shield and a hero-power orb beside it. Gwent's leader is a full painted card with a name and an ability button. Neither ever ships a hero without art.
- **Fix:** Ship art for every leader used by the beginner/default matchup, and replace `drawPlaceholderArt` inside the medallion with a designed fallback — a Current-tinted silhouette plate with the leader's initial, not a black dot. Separately, make the medallion an object: swap MeshBasicMaterial for MeshStandardMaterial so it sits inside ACES and picks up the new environment map, add a bevelled rim, a contact shadow, and a name plate under it.

### 4. [CRITICAL] The shadow map is fully configured and rendered every frame on the high tier, and nothing in the scene casts into it. `keyLight.castShadow = true` and `table.receiveShadow = true` are set, but `castShadow` appears nowhere else in the entire src tree — not on CardObject, not on LeaderObject, not on the plinths. So a 1024² PCFSoftShadowMap is allocated and re-rendered per frame producing an empty depth buffer, and no object on the board has any contact shadow.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:161-162, 296-306 and src/ui/battle/board.ts:141; grep for `castShadow` across src/ returns only scene.ts:297 and scene.ts:436
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1: "Contact shadow. Anything sitting on top of anything else casts. A panel floating with no shadow is a rectangle; a panel with a soft 24px drop and a tight 2px contact shadow is an object." Every card, every leader and both plinths currently float. It is also a §9 performance violation — a per-frame shadow pass with zero casters is a beautiful effect that costs frames and delivers nothing, on the tier that pays for it.
- **Fix:** Do not turn on `castShadow` for the cards — a shadow map is the wrong tool for a dozen flat quads. Add a shared `ContactShadow` helper: a soft-ellipse CanvasTexture quad at y=0.005 parented under every CardObject, LeaderObject and plinth, scaled to the object's footprint and darkened by its height. That works on the low tier too. Then delete the dead `shadowMap` configuration in scene.ts entirely, or wire it to a single real caster if one is ever added.

### 5. [MAJOR] `metalness` is set on the two most important materials in the scene and there is no environment map anywhere in the codebase. In three.js a metallic MeshStandardMaterial with no IBL reflects nothing and loses its diffuse in proportion to metalness — so `metalness: 0.38` on the mat throws away 38% of its diffuse response for zero specular return, and `metalness: 0.7` on the card rim throws away 70%. `envMapIntensity: 0.9` is set on the mat and multiplies a map that does not exist.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:135-137 (roughness 0.42, metalness 0.38, envMapIntensity 0.9) and src/ui/battle/cardMesh.ts:258-263 (metalness 0.7); `grep -rn "scene.environment|PMREMGenerator|envMap" src/` returns nothing
- **Why it fails:** This is the mechanical reason the mat is a dead slab and why the card rim comment ("thin edge so the card has physical thickness under raking light", cardMesh.ts:253) describes something that cannot happen. AAA-BAR §1 demands a lit surface with an edge treatment; a metal with nothing to reflect is darker and flatter than the same surface at metalness 0. MTG Arena's board reflects its own environment — you can see the room's warm light crawl along the brass inlays as the camera drifts.
- **Fix:** Add a memoised `getStudioEnvironment(renderer)` that PMREMs a canvas-drawn gradient cubemap and assigns it to `scene.environment`. Both PMREMGenerator and the canvas source are inside `three` and the browser — zero cost, zero new dependencies. Then the existing metalness values start paying for themselves, the mat picks up the backdrop's colour, and the card rim gets the raking specular its comment promises.

### 6. [MAJOR] There are two centre lines, 14px apart, in different colours and softness. The baked seam in the table texture sits at texture v=0.5, which is world z=0. The `midline` mesh sits at z=(-2.4+1.75)/2 = -0.325. Both run the full mat width. In crop-midline.png (1:1) you can see the crisp near-white 2px rule at y=36 and a separate soft violet band centred at y=50 below it.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:65-72 (baked seam, texture v=0.5) versus src/ui/battle/board.ts:169-175 (midline mesh at z=-0.325); scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/crop-midline.png
- **Why it fails:** Two dividers where the design intends one is the definition of unfinished. Worse, the surviving mesh is `PlaneGeometry(BOARD.width - 0.6, 0.055)` with a flat MeshBasicMaterial — constant brightness along its whole length, terminating in two square ends that butt straight into the mat's edge. AAA-BAR §7 names this verbatim: "Dividers that fade at the ends rather than butting hard into a panel edge." Gwent's row separator is a carved metal rail with a bevel, an inner shadow pressed into the mat, and ornamental end caps that taper to nothing.
- **Fix:** Delete the baked seam from `makeTableTexture()` and keep one line, at z=0. Give it a shared `fadeStripTexture(taper)` alphaMap so it fades to zero over the outer 18% at each end, and give it a real profile: a 1px `rgba(255,255,255,0.10)` highlight above, the colour body, and a 2px darkened inner shadow below, so it reads as a rail pressed into the surface rather than a stroke drawn on it.

### 7. [MAJOR] The four 'edge fade' rails outline the mat in neon instead of dissolving it, and they are misaligned with the mat by 0.25 units so there is a visible dark gap between each rail and the edge it is supposed to soften. The fade only covers the outer 22% of each strip (stops at 0 / 0.22 / 0.5 / 0.78 / 1); the middle 56% is flat, even, opaque violet at 55% alpha. In crop-mat-corner.png you can count three parallel lines within 15px at the top edge: the pink rail, a band of untouched backdrop, then the mat's own hard cut.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:90-106 (makeEdgeFadeTexture) and board.ts:146-166 (four rails positioned at ±(BOARD.depth/2 + 0.25) and ±(BOARD.width/2 + 0.25))
- **Why it fails:** The code comment at board.ts:80-88 states the intent correctly — "The reference has no such edge at all — its ground is a chamfered lozenge that dissolves into painted scenery" — and then the implementation does the opposite: it draws a bright even rule along 56% of each side, which is a crisper boundary than the geometry it was added to hide. AAA-BAR §1 bans "a `border: 1px solid` as the only edge treatment"; this is that, in neon, offset by a gap.
- **Fix:** Delete all four rails. The mat's own feathered alphaMap (defect 1) is the edge treatment. If a light seam at the arena boundary is wanted, put it *on* the mat as a wide additive gradient inside the mask, not as a separate quad floating 0.25 units outside it.

### 8. [MAJOR] The depth hierarchy is inverted. The backdrop is drawn with `MeshBasicMaterial`, `fog: false`, at full saturation, full sharpness and full contrast — the orange practicals on the left and right columns are the highest-luminance pixels in every frame I captured. Meanwhile `FogExp2(0x07040f, 0.028)` washes the *content* — the board cards, the mat, everything using a standard or fogged material — by roughly 37% toward near-black, and varies by only about 5 percentage points from the near edge of the mat to the far edge, so it delivers essentially no depth cue for a very large contrast cost.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:171 (FogExp2 density 0.028) and scene.ts:272-280 (backdrop MeshBasicMaterial with fog:false); most visible in scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/ultrawide.png where the backdrop occupies the outer 25% of each side
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §2: "Atmosphere — the furthest layer... always darkened and desaturated relative to the foreground." It is the opposite here: the atmosphere plane is at 100% and the content plane is fogged to ~63%. §6: "Saturation is a resource. The most saturated thing on screen should be the thing that matters most." The most saturated thing on screen is the wallpaper. Hearthstone's environment art is visibly value-crushed and desaturated relative to the minions; MTG Arena blurs and darkens its environment behind a real DOF falloff so the battlefield row is the only sharp thing.
- **Fix:** Grade the backdrop at upload time inside `setBackdrop`: draw it into the shrink canvas with a ~25% black multiply, a ~30% desaturation and a 2-3px blur before it becomes a texture (all canvas 2D, £0). Then drop the fog density to ~0.008 or remove it — let the mat's feathered mask and a new vignette pass carry depth instead of a flat 37% grey veil over the play area.

### 9. [MAJOR] There is no vignette, no film grain, no colour grade and no texture of any kind on any surface. The post chain is RenderPass → UnrealBloomPass → OutputPass and nothing else. The mat's only surface detail is a hex line pattern at 11% alpha that is perfectly uniform in brightness from the mat's top edge to its bottom edge, with no noise, no dust, no scuff and no grain. At 1:1 in crop-mat-corner.png it is indistinguishable from a CSS `background-image` hex tile.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:341-357 (composer passes) and src/ui/battle/board.ts:25-78 (makeTableTexture — radial gradient, hex strokes, seam, and nothing else); `grep -rni "vignette|grain|ShaderPass" src/` returns nothing
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1: "Texture. Film grain, noise, brushed metal, fabric weave, dust, condensation — something at 2-6% opacity so the surface is not mathematically smooth. Perfectly clean gradients read as CSS. Reality has dirt." The mat is mathematically smooth. And with no vignette, the four corners of the frame are the brightest region, so the composition actively pushes the eye off the board and onto the scenery. Hearthstone's board is covered in scuffs, wood grain, spilled wax and dust motes; Gwent runs a permanent parchment grain and a heavy corner vignette.
- **Fix:** Write one hand-rolled `GradePass` (a single ShaderPass — the class is inside `three`, no new dependency) after the bloom: radial vignette, a 2-3% animated grain, and a slight shoulder/toe curve. Add a shared `makeNoiseTexture()` canvas generator and multiply it into `makeTableTexture()` at ~4%, plus a scuff/dust layer. Gate the grain animation on `prefers-reduced-motion` and skip the whole pass on the low tier.

### 10. [MAJOR] The camera is orthographic, and that is the wrong call at this bar. A parallel projection of an axis-aligned box is an axis-aligned screen rectangle — so the mat is geometrically incapable of ever reading as a surface in a room. It also delivers zero parallax (an orthographic camera has no perspective divide, so nothing behind can move relative to anything in front), and the backdrop is parented to the camera at scene.ts:286, which pins it absolutely. The card thickness rim of 0.02 units projects to about 0.17px at the current 10.4-degree tilt, so it is invisible. And the painted backdrop has a strong receding perspective converging up-and-centre while the mat insists you are looking straight down at a table: the two projections openly contradict each other, which is the deepest reason the frame reads as a panel on a photograph.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:185-188 (VIEW_HEIGHT 19.4, OrthographicCamera(-10,10,6.7,-6.7,0.1,140) at (0,24,4.4)) and scene.ts:286 (camera.add(backdropMesh))
- **Why it fails:** The rationale at scene.ts:176-184 (no trapezoid, uniform token size, hit-testing matches) does not survive contact with §2 and §3. §2 requires separation "carried by blur, desaturation, scale and parallax" — orthographic forecloses scale and parallax outright. §3 requires idle life and secondary motion, which for a board scene means a slow camera drift with the world moving behind it. All three reference games are perspective: Hearthstone uses a mild perspective so the board's far rim converges and the near rim wraps toward you; MTG Arena uses a stronger tilt with real foreshortening and DOF; Gwent parallaxes its frame against its background as the mouse moves. The convergence is what tells the eye "surface in a place".
- **Fix:** Swap to `PerspectiveCamera(24, aspect, 0.1, 200)` and solve the distance from VIEW_HEIGHT: `d = (VIEW_HEIGHT/2) / tan(fov/2)`, keeping the same tilt. At 24 degrees across 17.6 units of board depth, token scale varies by roughly ±8% front-to-back — which is desirable, since it makes your own row read as nearer — and off-centre lean at the row extremes stays under 4 degrees. `Raycaster.setFromCamera` handles perspective identically, so the hit-testing concern is a non-issue. Then unparent the backdrop from the camera onto a world-space plane behind the mat so it can finally parallax, and match its painted horizon to the mat's far edge.

### 11. [MAJOR] The two location plinths are unlabelled wireframe rectangles floating in empty mat, and both are on the right-hand side, so the board is visibly asymmetric — two empty boxes stacked on the right, nothing on the left, and roughly 25% of the mat's area (everything left of x=-5 world) is dead space containing nothing at all.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:274-307 (both sides use `x = BOARD.width/2 - 1.5`; a 1.5x1.9 plate at 35% opacity plus a 4-segment RingGeometry at 30%); visible at (1218,160) and (1218,676) in scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/board.png
- **Why it fails:** They read as UI someone forgot to finish — a 1px violet rectangle outline with nothing in it, no icon, no label, no explanation of what it is for. AAA-BAR §5: "Empty states are designed too." §7: consistent corner radii and a single icon grid. MTG Arena's graveyard and exile zones are physical card-back stacks with a count badge and a hover-peek; Hearthstone simply has no empty zone visible on the board at all. Neither ships a bare wireframe.
- **Fix:** Make it a physical object: a recessed inlay in the mat with a bevelled lip, an engraved Current crest at ~8% opacity, an inner shadow, and a contact shadow — so an empty plinth reads as an empty socket rather than an unstyled div. Mirror the two plinths (player right, enemy left, or both centred outboard of the rows) so the composition is balanced, and fill the dead left apron with something — a Reactions rail, the deck stack, or simply pull `BOARD.width` in so there is no apron to fill.

### 12. [MAJOR] Board cards are unlit, un-tone-mapped, effectively zero-thickness decals. Front and back are `MeshBasicMaterial` with `toneMapped: false`, so they receive no light and bypass ACES entirely — they sit in a different tonal space from the mat they lie on. The 'thin edge' rim is `BoxGeometry(w*1.005, h*1.005, 0.02)`, which at the current 46px-per-world-unit scale is 0.93px before the camera tilt and about 0.17px after it. No shadow, no rim light, no contact.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/cardMesh.ts:236-251 (MeshBasicMaterial, toneMapped:false) and cardMesh.ts:254-266 (0.02-unit rim, metalness 0.7 with no envMap)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1 (contact shadow, edge treatment) and §2 (the content plane must be separable from the midground). In midgame.png the three enemy cards look like stickers laid on wallpaper: identical brightness, no ground contact, no glow spill onto the mat despite carrying saturated Cinder-orange and Tide-blue frames. Hearthstone renders a board minion as a *different object* from a hand card — a circular framed portrait with a shadow ellipse, an idle breathing loop and a rim light. MTG Arena keeps the card silhouette but adds a genuine drop shadow, a visible thickness bevel, and a specular sweep as the camera moves.
- **Fix:** Give each board card a contact-shadow quad (defect 4), thicken the rim to ~0.06 units and light it with the new environment map, and let the card's Current colour spill onto the mat via a small additive gradient quad beneath it. Consider dropping `toneMapped: false` on the board (keep it for hand cards if crispness demands) so the card and the mat share one tone curve.

### 13. [MINOR] The hex texture is a 1024x1024 square canvas mapped onto a 21x17.6 box face, so every hexagon is stretched 1.19:1 horizontally. Combined with the uniform 11%-alpha stroke and the total absence of a normal or bump map, the pattern reads as a flat wallpaper tile that the two coloured light pools slide over without ever catching a ridge.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:25-78 (makeTableTexture, size=1024 square) applied to board.ts:132 BoxGeometry(21, 0.42, 17.6)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1's texture requirement and §7's craft details. A real surface pattern catches light. Gwent's board is a physically-shaded stone slab where the carved lines have depth and pick up the rim light; Hearthstone's board planks have grain that changes value under the tavern light.
- **Fix:** Author the texture at the mat's actual aspect (e.g. 1024x858) or set `texture.repeat` to compensate, and generate a matching normalMap from the same hex path (a canvas-drawn height field converted to normals, ~30 lines, no dependency) so the hex ridges catch the key and the two rim lights. Add the shared noise layer at 4%.

### 14. [MINOR] Camera framing is a fixed 19.4 world units of height regardless of viewport size, with no floor and no safe areas. At 844x390 (phone landscape, an explicitly supported target) one world unit is 20px: the leader medallion is 44px tall and is completely hidden behind the RIVAL·OBSESSION HUD chip, and the board card is ~69px tall and illegible. At 1280x720 the mat's bottom edge and the player leader both fall behind the hand.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:185 (VIEW_HEIGHT = 19.4, a constant) and scene.ts:377-393 (resize only adjusts width and clamps zoom for narrow viewports); see scripts/screenshots/recon/battle3d/phone-landscape.png and board-720.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §9: "Every screen still has to work at 1280x720 and on a phone in landscape." It does not. The leader — the object the whole match is about — is occluded at the minimum supported size.
- **Fix:** Solve VIEW_HEIGHT from viewport height with a floor (e.g. shrink the framed depth below ~500px tall so world units grow), and reserve top and bottom safe-area insets in the camera framing so the HUD strips can never overlap a leader. Publish those insets so the HUD layer can read them rather than guessing.

### 15. [MINOR] The scene's only idle motion is a sine wobble on two point-light intensities, and it is not gated on reduced-motion. `render()` unconditionally drives `rimWarm.intensity` and `rimCool.intensity` every frame. Elsewhere in the same subsystem reduced-motion is respected (vfx.ts:98, presenter.ts:163), so this is an inconsistency, not a policy.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/scene.ts:402-405
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §3: "`prefers-reduced-motion` must kill the decorative layer and keep the functional one. That is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have." §9 restates accessibility as non-negotiable. Separately, §3's "idle is never dead" is met only nominally — a ±5 intensity wobble on two off-centre blobs is not a board that breathes.
- **Fix:** Read `getSettings().reducedMotion` in `render()` and pin the intensities to their midpoints when set. Then build real idle life that also respects it: a very slow camera drift (needs the perspective camera), a specular crawl across the mat, and a few slow motes in the fx layer, all on 3-8 second periods per §3.

### 16. [MINOR] Idle slot rings ease to opacity 0, so the board never communicates its own shape. `update()` sets `targetRing = marker.highlighted ? ... : 0`, meaning the six slots per side are completely invisible until a drag begins. With three units on a 21-unit-wide mat (midgame.png) the row reads as three isolated islands floating in undifferentiated purple with no sense of how many more will fit.

- **Where:** src/ui/battle/board.ts:198-232 (ring creation) and board.ts:319-331 (update, targetRing = 0 at rest)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §5 ("Empty states are designed too") and §6 ("Squint at the screen: it should resolve into clear light and dark masses. If everything is mid-purple, nothing reads"). Squinting at midgame.png the mat is one giant mid-purple mass with three small chips on it. Hearthstone shows no slots but its board is sized so a full seven-minion row fills it edge to edge, so the emptiness is legible as space rather than as void; Gwent draws a permanent row frame with a bevelled inner edge.
- **Fix:** Either give the rows a permanent, very low-contrast ground treatment — a subtly darker inlaid band per row with a bevelled lip and a contact shadow, so the row exists as a structure whether or not it is occupied — or narrow BOARD.width so a full row genuinely fills the ground. Keep the bright ring purely for the drag-target state.

## Plan

1. Swap OrthographicCamera for PerspectiveCamera (fov ~24, distance solved from VIEW_HEIGHT, same tilt) in scene.ts:186, and re-derive fitBackdrop for a perspective frustum. Nothing else on this list can reach a 9 while the projection guarantees a screen-aligned rectangle.
2. Unparent the backdrop from the camera (scene.ts:286) onto a world-space plane behind the mat, matched to the painted horizon, so it can parallax against the board.
3. Replace the mat BoxGeometry with a subdivided PlaneGeometry carrying a shared feathered-superellipse alphaMap, and delete the four neon edge rails (board.ts:131-166) in the same commit. This is the headline fix.
4. Rebuild the light rig: ambient 1.5 -> ~0.35, add scene.environment from a canvas-built PMREM cubemap, give the two rim point lights visible emitter objects, and re-bake makeTableTexture's gradient to agree with the key light direction instead of being centre-out.
5. Add contact shadows everywhere: a shared soft-ellipse quad primitive under every CardObject, LeaderObject and plinth. Then delete the dead shadow-map configuration in scene.ts, which currently renders an empty depth pass every frame.
6. Grade the backdrop on upload (25% darken, 30% desaturate, 2-3px blur inside shrinkForTexture) and cut fog density from 0.028 to ~0.008 or remove it, so the content plane is the sharpest and most saturated thing in frame rather than the wallpaper.
7. Resolve the double centre line: delete the baked seam from makeTableTexture, keep one rail at z=0, give it a shared end-taper alphaMap and a real bevel profile (highlight above, body, inner shadow below).
8. Rebuild the leader: ship or design a real fallback portrait, move the medallion to MeshStandardMaterial inside the tone map, add a bevelled rim lit by the new environment, a contact shadow, a name plate, and scale it to roughly 1.6x a board card so the win condition is not the smallest object on the board.
9. Give board cards presence: thicken the rim to ~0.06 units, drop toneMapped:false so they share the mat's tone curve, add a Current-coloured light spill quad beneath each, and add an idle breathe.
10. Redesign the location plinths as recessed inlays with an engraved crest and an inner shadow, mirror them so the board is symmetric, and either use or remove the dead 25% of mat on the left.
