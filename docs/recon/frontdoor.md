# Recon: Front door — lobby, play (mode select), starter, sign-in, queue

**Score: 4/10** — 21 defects (5 critical)

## The single worst thing

There is exactly one surface material in the entire front door, and every object is made of it. `--glass` (rgba(28,20,48,0.62)) + `1px solid --glass-border` + `backdrop-filter: blur(18px)` is the identity chip, both currency chips, the deck widget, all nine lobby lozenges, all three rail panels, all fourteen mode cards, the sign-in panel and the queue panel. Thirty objects, one recipe, no light source, no texture, no contact shadow — so nothing on screen has a rank, a weight, or a material. That is why it reads as an admin dashboard with a purple theme rather than a game: in Hearthstone the Play button is carved stone with a lit rim, the quest log is parchment, and the rank chip is enamelled metal — three materials you can name by touch. Fixing the material hierarchy reframes every screen in this domain at once; the icon set, the flat PLAY button and the dead left column are all downstream of it.

## Defects

### 1. [CRITICAL] One material for thirty objects. `.panel`, `.btn`, `.mode-card`, `.lobby-deck-widget`, `.currency` and `.lobby-identity` all resolve to the same translucent purple wash with a 1px lilac hairline and no directional light. Nothing carries a key-light gradient, a rim highlight on the lit edge, an inner shadow on the unlit edge, or a contact shadow. The lobby's nine nav tiles, the right rail's three panels and the deck widget are the same object at four sizes.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:311-318 (.panel), base.css:343-361 (.btn), src/ui/theme/screens.css:394-404 (.mode-card), screens.css:340-347 (.lobby-deck-widget), screens.css:110-119, 144-154
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1 'Nothing is flat' — a gradient with one consistent key light, an edge treatment, texture and a contact shadow are the minimum on every surface; §1 bans 'a border: 1px solid as the only edge treatment'. It also fails §2, which demands four resolvable depth planes: this screen has two (radial wash, content). Hearthstone gives the main menu three distinct materials (carved wood frame, lit brass button, inset slate panel) so rank is legible before you read a word; here rank is legible only from font size.
- **Fix:** Add a materials layer with three named recipes and no free-form glass: `--mat-hero` (linear-gradient 170deg from a 12%-lighter top to a 8%-darker bottom, inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.16) top rim, inset 0 -1px 0 rgba(0,0,0,0.5) bottom, 0 18px 40px rgba(0,0,0,0.55) + 0 2px 0 rgba(0,0,0,0.8) contact), `--mat-panel` (same light, half amplitude, recessed: inset top shadow instead of rim), `--mat-chip` (no shadow, rim only). Every consumer in these five screens switches to one of the three. Key light is fixed at top-left for the whole app so the highlight direction never disagrees between two adjacent tiles.

### 2. [CRITICAL] The icon system is three unrelated art languages sharing one 2-column grid. In the lobby: Collection `▦` and Events `✦` and Inbox `✉` are Unicode text glyphs at hairline weight; Merch Drops, Missions, Mastery and Hype Wave are raster PNGs in different styles (Missions is a black phone with a magenta tick — a device with a UI inside it; Hype Wave is a gradient bar chart with a sparkle); Achievements is a full-colour purple 3D trophy with a black outline that is the most saturated pixel on the screen. Mastery's laurel PNG is dark grey on a dark panel and is effectively invisible at 1600×900. On the play screen all fourteen icons are Unicode glyphs from `MODES[]`, and `◈` (Practice vs AI) and `◇` (Casual Match) are the same shape — two modes with an indistinguishable icon. `⚗` and `☠` fall through to the emoji font, so The Lab and Weekly Boss render at a different weight and colour from their twelve siblings, and on any machine missing those code points they render as tofu.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/lobbyScreen.ts:163-193; src/ui/screens/playScreen.ts:66-117; src/ui/theme/screens.css:200-240 (.ui-icon), 353-354; see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/lobby-nav-zoom.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §7: 'Icons on a single grid, single stroke weight, single optical size.' Two of three currently fail, and one icon fails contrast outright. MTG Arena's home nav is one 24px stroke set at a single weight, tinted by state not by artist; Gwent's menu glyphs are a single engraved-metal family. Shipping typeface glyphs as icons also breaks the £0/no-dependency constraint's implicit promise of determinism — the icon is whatever font the player's OS happens to have.
- **Fix:** Author one hand-drawn SVG sprite (24px grid, 1.75px stroke, 2px optical padding, single `currentColor` fill) covering the 9 lobby destinations, the 14 modes, both currencies and the gear. Ship it as an inlined symbol sheet in a `src/ui/art/uiIcons.ts` (no runtime dep, no network). Delete every Unicode glyph and every per-icon PNG from these two files. Give Practice and Casual distinct marks. Icons get one tint token at rest and one at hover; nothing is full-colour except the tier crest.

### 3. [CRITICAL] The lobby is physically broken at 1280×720, the stated minimum. Measured in-page: `.lobby-screen.scrollHeight = 822`, `clientHeight = 720`, and both `body` and `.lobby-screen` are `overflow: hidden`, so 102px is clipped with no way to reach it. `#lobby-inbox` sits at top=714/bottom=822 — the Inbox button, badged with 8 unread, is entirely unreachable. `#lobby-news` is cut mid-sentence at 'and the'. The leader's Current badge is sliced in half. Worst, `.lobby-footer` occupies y=683-720 while the nav grid runs to y=822, so the footer text is drawn straight through the Achievements and Events tiles and their badges.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/screens.css:64 (.lobby-screen{overflow:hidden}), src/ui/theme/base.css:204-219 (body{overflow:hidden}), screens.css:288-298 (.lobby-body grid, align-items:center), screens.css:373; see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/lobby-720-probe.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §9 hard constraint: 'Every screen still has to work at 1280×720 and on a phone in landscape.' Not 'is tight at' — works at. Hearthstone's menu reflows its quest rail and shrinks the hero plate; nothing is ever cut off with no scroll. Text drawn on top of buttons is a defect no shipped studio game contains.
- **Fix:** Make `.lobby-body` a height-driven layout rather than a fixed three-column grid with centred items: give the nav grid `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(0,1fr))` with a `--tile-h: clamp(56px, 8.4vh, 76px)` and let the leader stage use `height: min(52vh, 520px)`. Move the footer out of flow into the header rail (see the dev-string defect) so it can never overlap. Add a `container`-query or a 760px-height breakpoint that drops the nav to 3 columns × 3 rows. Assert it: extend scripts/verify-screens.mjs to fail when any lobby child's bottom exceeds the viewport at 1280×720.

### 4. [CRITICAL] On a phone in landscape (844×390) the lobby loses two thirds of itself. The right rail is `display: none` by media query, so record, daily missions and news vanish entirely rather than reflowing. Only two of nine nav tiles (Collection, Deck Builder) are on screen; Missions, Merch Drops, Mastery, Hype Wave, Achievements, Events and Inbox are below a hard clip with no scroll. The leader card is cropped through the middle. The footer dev string is drawn over the Collection tile. The wordmark is hidden below 900px so there is no brand mark at all.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/screens.css:376-379 (@media max-width:1100px { .lobby-rail { display: none } }), screens.css:185-189 (wordmark hidden <900px), screens.css:64; see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/lobby-landscape-844.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §9: 'on a phone in landscape' is a hard constraint, and §5 'Every state is designed' extends to every viewport. Hearthstone Mobile does not delete the quest log on a phone — it collapses it to a badged tab. Deleting a feature is not a responsive strategy.
- **Fix:** Below 1100px, collapse the rail into a badged drawer button in the header rail rather than removing it, and switch the nav from a 2-column grid to a horizontally scrollable rail of the same tiles with snap points. Compress the leader stage to a portrait-crop strip. Keep the wordmark as a mark-only lockup (no wordmark) at small widths instead of nothing.

### 5. [CRITICAL] The email and password fields on the sign-in screen have no border. `.signin-field input` sets `border: 1px solid var(--line)` and `--line` is not defined anywhere in the codebase, so the shorthand is invalid at computed-value time and the whole border is dropped. Verified live: computed `border-style: none`, `border-width: 0px`. The result is two unlabelled black rectangles floating on a dark panel — the player has to guess where to type. The same undefined variable is used again in battle.css:1110.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/screens.css:3783 (and src/ui/theme/battle.css:1110); rendered proof in scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/signin-states.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1 (an edge treatment is mandatory) and §5 (every state designed — this control has no rest state at all). It also fails §9 accessibility: a form field with no boundary and no filled contrast against its container is not discoverable. Arena's login fields are inset wells with a lit top-inner edge and a visible floor; you can see the hole before you click it.
- **Fix:** Replace with the shared inset-field recipe: `background: linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(0,0,0,0.42), rgba(0,0,0,0.24))`, `box-shadow: inset 0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,0.6), inset 0 -1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.06)`, `border: 1px solid rgba(180,160,255,0.22)`. Then grep the tree for every `var(--)` with no definition and add a build-time check — an undefined custom property fails silently and this one shipped.

### 6. [MAJOR] Hover is a 2px nudge and nothing else. `.btn:hover` moves the element up 2px, brightens the border and adds a glow that is invisible over the existing purple; `.lobby-nav-btn` inherits it verbatim; `.mode-card:hover` is the same at 3px; `.lobby-deck-widget:hover` only changes `border-color`. In the forced-hover capture, the hovered Collection tile is essentially indistinguishable from the resting Missions tile beside it, and hovering the 500px-wide PLAY button produces no visible change whatsoever.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:363-371, 385-387; src/ui/theme/screens.css:405-409 (.mode-card:hover), 348 (.lobby-deck-widget:hover), 120 (.lobby-identity:hover); see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/lobby-hover-focus.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §5: 'A hover that only changes opacity is not a hover. The reference games move the element, light it, sound it and often scale it — all three at once, in under 120ms.' In Hearthstone every menu button pushes forward, its rim brightens, a light sweeps across it and it clicks audibly on the same frame. Here the pointer gets a hairline.
- **Fix:** One hover recipe applied through the material layer: `transform: translateY(-2px) scale(1.015)`, key-light amplitude doubled (the top rim goes from 0.16 to 0.30), the icon tints to the accent, the contact shadow tightens and darkens, and a 140ms specular wipe crosses the tile. 110ms in with `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.8,0.2,1)`, 180ms out with ease-in. Pair with the existing `sfx.ui.hover` on the same frame.

### 7. [MAJOR] Focus-visible is the browser-default white rectangle, and it has the wrong radius. The global rule forces `border-radius: var(--radius-sm)` (6px) on the outline, so on the pill-shaped nav lozenges (999px radius) the white ring cuts straight across all four corners of the tile it is meant to be indicating — clearly visible on the Deck Builder tile in the forced-focus capture. On the sign-in password field the same 3px stark-white box is the only focus treatment.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:157-165 (:root[data-keyboard-nav=true] :focus-visible { outline: 3px solid #ffffff; border-radius: var(--radius-sm) }), base.css:235-239; screens.css:3790; see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/lobby-hover-focus.png and signin-states.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §5: 'Focus-visible must be beautiful, not the browser default ring. It is on screen every time somebody plays with a keyboard.' Arena's keyboard focus is a gold inner bevel plus an outer glow that follows the control's own silhouette. A 6px-radius white rectangle around a 999px-radius pill is the tell that nobody has ever tabbed through this screen.
- **Fix:** Drop the hard-coded radius and use `border-radius: inherit` on a `::after` pseudo-ring so the focus follows each control's real silhouette. Replace the flat white with a two-stop ring: `0 0 0 2px var(--bg-void), 0 0 0 4px var(--accent-bright), 0 0 18px rgba(217,165,255,0.45)`. Keep the 3/2/5px width tokens for the thin/medium/thick setting. Contrast still passes because the inner dark ring guarantees separation on any background.

### 8. [MAJOR] The unclaimed-count badges sit in the wrong place because of a layout accident, not a decision. `.lobby-nav-badge` uses `margin-left: auto` inside `.lobby-nav-btn`, which is `flex-direction: column` — so instead of floating to the top-right corner it becomes a third stacked row and lands at the bottom-right, overlapping the lozenge's curved edge. In the 2x capture the Achievements '1' and Inbox '8' hang half off the visual mass of their tiles, and at 1280×720 the '1' collides with the footer text.

- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §7 (craft details nobody asks for and everybody notices) and §1 (anything sitting on top of anything else casts). Hearthstone's unclaimed-quest badge is a pinned, rimmed, shadowed disc on the top-right corner of the plate with a subtle pop-in; it never touches text and it never sits on a curve.
- **Fix:** `position: absolute; top: 8px; right: 10px` on the badge, `position: relative` on the tile. Give it a real object treatment: `background: radial-gradient(circle at 35% 30%, #ff8fd8, var(--accent-hot))`, a 1px dark rim, a `0 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,0.6)` contact shadow, tabular numerals, and a 220ms scale-overshoot entrance so it never pops in (§7).

### 9. [MAJOR] A development string is shipped as product chrome. The lobby footer reads 'HYPEBOUND · offline build · your art and music drop into public/assets/' — a note to the developer, centred under the main menu, present in every screenshot, and at 1280×720 and 844×390 it is drawn on top of the interactive tiles.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/lobbyScreen.ts:257-259; src/ui/theme/screens.css:373-374

### 10. [MAJOR] The queue — the game's single longest wait — is a paragraph of text. 'Connecting…' is `--fs-xl` body copy in a static panel; there is no loader, no motion, no world. Two thirds of the screen (the whole left third, right third and lower half) is empty near-black. The AI-offer box inside it is a plain 6px-radius rectangle with a flat `1px solid var(--accent-gold)` border nested inside a 20px-radius panel, so the two radii visibly disagree.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/queueScreen.ts:53-77; src/ui/theme/screens.css:3799-3811, 3758-3767; see scripts/screenshots/recon/lobby/queue-forced.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §7: 'Loading that is part of the world, not a spinner' — this is worse than a spinner, it is a sentence. §3: 'Idle is never dead.' §2: one depth plane. Gwent's queue keeps the leader animated on a lit plinth with the crowd noise up; Arena runs the planeswalker idle and a slowly filling searchlight. The honesty of the real player count is good writing and should stay — it just needs a stage to be said on.
- **Fix:** Put the player's leader on the stage, lit, breathing, with the venue backdrop behind at 60% blur. Replace 'Connecting…' with a world-native progress element: a slow neon searchlight arc sweeping the room on a 4s period whose speed is unchanged by wait time (so it never implies progress it does not have), with the real 'N players searching' number set in tabular numerals underneath. Give the AI-offer box `border-radius: calc(var(--radius-lg) - var(--sp-4))` and the panel material rather than a hairline, and animate it in with the shared stagger helper.

### 11. [MAJOR] The mode select is a spreadsheet. Fourteen identical 12px-radius cards in an `auto-fill` grid with `align-content: start`, all the same size, all the same material, all with the same 52px purple square icon plate — and twelve of them carry the identical green word 'READY', which is a status label that conveys nothing because it is never absent. At 1600×900 the grid stops after 3.5 rows and leaves a 340px band of dead black across the bottom; the wider the display, the more dead space. There is no hero, no ordering by importance, and no art anywhere.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/playScreen.ts:65-118 (MODES), 134; src/ui/theme/screens.css:385-392 (.mode-grid), 394-441; see play-1600.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §6 'Accent restraint — one hero accent per screen' (twelve equal green accents is the opposite) and §2 (one depth plane, no atmosphere, no midground). Hearthstone's Play screen is two enormous illustrated plates; Arena's format select is full-bleed key art per format with the featured event twice the size of the rest; Gwent's is a vertical stack of painted banners. None of the three present their modes as a table.
- **Fix:** Break the uniform grid: a hero tile (Casual/Practice, whichever is live) at 2×2 with a bleeding art crop, a second tier of 4 medium tiles, and the long tail as a compact list. Drop 'READY' entirely — a mode that is playable needs no label; keep the status text only for 'Coming online' and the Grand Tour's '1 of 10'. Give each mode a distinct icon (see the icon defect) and a per-mode accent tint on the icon plate only. Fill the bottom band by letting the hero tile grow with `min-content` rows rather than `align-content: start`.

### 12. [MAJOR] The starter picker — the first screen a new player ever sees — is a listbox. Eleven identical dark rectangles, each carrying a 10px coloured dot and two lines of text, next to a mostly empty detail panel. There is no faction art, no crest, no colour identity beyond the dot. The list overflows the viewport at both 1600×900 (Viral Influencers clipped) and 1280×720 (cut at Neon Idols), and `.starter-screen` uses bare `overflow-y: auto` without the styled `.scroll` class, so it gets the OS scrollbar. `render()` rewrites `root.innerHTML` wholesale on every faction click, so: the change is a hard cut with zero transition, the ambient background's 40s drift animation restarts on every click, the whole card canvas is re-rendered, and the scroll position resets to the top — pick the tenth faction and the list jumps back to the first. The confirm button carries `btn-lg`, a class that does not exist in any stylesheet, so the game's most consequential button is a stock 44px pill.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/starterScreen.ts:37-101 (render rewrites innerHTML), :84 (btn-lg, undefined); src/ui/theme/screens.css:1981 (.starter-screen overflow-y:auto, no .scroll), 1994-2021; see starter-raw.png and starter-1280x720.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §5 'Empty states are designed too… a moment to be charming', §7 'Nothing ever pops in. Anything appearing has an entrance' and 'a default OS scrollbar in a fantasy card game is a tear in the world', §9 (must work at 1280×720). Hearthstone's class pick is ten illustrated hero portraits that light and speak when you hover; Arena's colour pick is five painted mana plates; Gwent's faction select is full-bleed banner art with the leader animating in. A radio list is what this game currently offers instead.
- **Fix:** Replace the list with a horizontal carousel of eleven faction plates carrying the crest, the faction colour as a real gradient and a cropped leader portrait; keyboard arrows move the selection. Stop rewriting innerHTML — mutate only the detail panel's text nodes and cross-fade the card with the shared 260ms transition, keeping scroll position and the background animation intact. Define `.btn-lg` (min-height 60px, --fs-lg, +0.04em tracking) or give the confirm button `.btn-play`'s treatment. Add `.scroll` to the container.

### 13. [MAJOR] There is no texture anywhere in the domain. The only non-flat element in the whole front door is a 64px grid drawn at `rgba(255,255,255,0.028)` and masked away before it reaches the edges. Every panel fill, every button fill, the PLAY gradient and the background wash are mathematically smooth. The PLAY button in particular is a bare `linear-gradient(135deg, #b56cff, #ff5fa2)` — the most important object on the screen is two-stop CSS with a white sheen sliding across it.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:275-307 (.ambient-bg + ::after grid), base.css:378-383 (.btn-primary), base.css:389-415 (.btn-play)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §1: 'Texture. Film grain, noise, brushed metal, fabric weave, dust, condensation — something at 2–6% opacity so the surface is not mathematically smooth. Perfectly clean gradients read as CSS. Reality has dirt.' Hearthstone's main button is painted wood with visible grain and chipped paint; Gwent's panels carry a parchment weave. HYPEBOUND's nightlife premise gives free licence to bloom, haze, condensation and CRT grain and uses none of it.
- **Fix:** Generate a 128×128 tiling noise tile once at boot on an offscreen canvas (no dependency, no asset, ~1ms), expose it as a CSS custom property data-URI, and multiply it over every material at 3-5% with `background-blend-mode: overlay`. For the PLAY button specifically: three stops rather than two, a 12% inner top rim, a darker 8% inner bottom edge, the noise tile, a soft 0 20px 44px accent-tinted bloom underneath, and a 2px contact shadow so it sits on the screen instead of floating.

### 14. [MAJOR] The value structure collapses in the lower two thirds. All three ambient radial gradients are anchored to the top of the viewport (`at 15% 0%`, `at 85% 10%`), so the lobby resolves under a squint into one bright band across the top 25% and a dark void below. Because the nav tiles are translucent white washes rather than opaque surfaces, their apparent contrast depends entirely on what is behind them: Collection at the top of the grid reads clearly, while Events and Inbox at the bottom nearly disappear into the backdrop. The same component reads at four different contrasts in one column. The play screen and sign-in screen have the same problem more severely — below y≈300 they are near-flat #0a0714.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:279-284 (.ambient-bg), base.css:355 (.btn background: white-on-transparent wash), src/ui/theme/screens.css:66-79 (.lobby-bg::before); see lobby-nav-zoom.png (Events/Inbox vs Collection)
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §6: 'Value structure. Squint at the screen: it should resolve into clear light and dark masses. If everything is mid-purple, nothing reads.' And §1's requirement of ONE consistent key light per screen — a translucent tile inherits its value from its position, which means the screen has as many suns as it has tiles. Gwent's panels are opaque and lit identically wherever they sit.
- **Fix:** Make the tile fill opaque (or near-opaque) and independent of the backdrop, then apply the key-light gradient on top of it, so a tile at the bottom of the grid has the same value as one at the top. Rebalance the atmosphere: add a bottom-anchored cool wash and a vignette so the composition has a dark floor rather than a black hole, and put the brightest ambient value directly behind the leader stage so the left column stops reading as empty.

### 15. [MAJOR] The lobby's hero stage reuses the deck-list card renderer as a hero portrait. `renderCardToCanvas(leader, 300)` puts a 300px trading card — complete with cost pip, LEADER type line, four rarity diamonds and body copy at roughly 8px — where a hero should be. The rules text is illegible at that size, the '30' health pip is meaningless on a menu, the 'PULSE' chip covers the character's face, and the card frame crops the art to a 200px window. That, plus the fact that the stage column occupies a third of the screen, is the 'dead zone on the left'.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/lobbyScreen.ts:137-148, :262-265; src/ui/theme/screens.css:300-336
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §2 (the content plane should be the thing the player is reading, at the right scale) and §4 (type hierarchy — 8px unreadable body copy in a hero slot is not a hierarchy). Hearthstone's menu hero is a large framed portrait with an idle animation and no game stats; Arena's home screen is full-bleed planeswalker key art. Neither shows you a shrunken list item.
- **Fix:** Add a `renderLeaderPortrait()` path to the card renderer that draws art only — no cost pip, no rules box, no type line — at a taller 3:4 crop with a lit frame, a floor reflection and a soft rim light from the lobby key direction. Scale it to `min(52vh, 520px)`. Keep the faction crest and the Current chip below it as separate chips so nothing covers the face.

### 16. [MAJOR] Idle life is one floating card and one sheen. Three frames 900ms apart are pixel-identical except for the leader card's 6s float and the PLAY sheen's position; the lobby-glow's `translateX(6%)` over 14s is imperceptible and the background pulse is a 4% scale on an already-diffuse gradient. Nothing else on the screen moves, ever: no motes, no specular crawl on any panel, no reaction on the tiles, and the leader's drop-shadow is welded to the card so it rotates with it instead of staying planted.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/screens.css:66-98 (lobby-pulse, lobby-sweep), 308-319 (leader-float + static drop-shadow); src/ui/theme/base.css:399-415 (sheen); see lobby-idle-0.png vs lobby-idle-2.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §3: 'Idle is never dead. A screen with nothing happening still breathes: slow ambient drift, a gentle specular crawl, motes.' And 'Secondary motion — when the main thing moves, something small moves because of it… the single biggest gap between animated and alive.' In Hearthstone's menu the tavern fire flickers, the sign swings and dust drifts through the light; every reference game's front door has three or four independent slow loops.
- **Fix:** Three cheap ambient loops honouring reduced-motion: (1) 14 CSS-only motes on 9-14s randomised drift paths within the stage column; (2) a specular highlight that crawls across the nav grid on a 7s period, offset per tile by index so the tiles do not pulse in unison; (3) decouple the leader's shadow from its transform — keep it planted and let it soften/stretch as the card rises. All amplitudes under 4% so nothing distracts, all killed by `[data-reduced-motion="true"]` like the existing loops.

### 17. [MAJOR] Screen entrances and element entrances do not exist below the screen level. Every screen fades in with one 280ms `screen-in` on the root and every child arrives on the same frame — nine nav tiles, three rail panels, the leader and the PLAY button all appear simultaneously. There is no stagger anywhere in the domain.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:256-273 (.screen / @keyframes screen-in); no per-child animation in lobbyScreen.ts, playScreen.ts, starterScreen.ts
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §3: 'Everything overlaps. Sequential animations that wait for each other feel like a slideshow. Stagger by 30-60ms per element.' Arena's home screen lands the hero first, then the nav rail sweeps in element by element; the difference is the whole feeling of the app booting versus a page loading.
- **Fix:** A `stagger(elements, {step: 40, base: 60})` helper that writes `--enter-delay` onto each child and a single shared `@keyframes enter-rise` (translateY(14px) + opacity, `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.8,0.2,1)`, 320ms). Apply it to the nav grid, the rail panels, the mode grid and the starter list. One line per screen; reduced-motion collapses every delay to 0.

### 18. [MINOR] Three corner radii and two button silhouettes fight on the lobby: the nav tiles and PLAY and the chips are `--radius-pill` (999px, so a 76px tile becomes a lozenge), the rail panels are `--radius-lg` (20px), the deck widget is `--radius` (12px), the mode cards are also 12px and the queue's offer box is 6px. Adjacent objects at the same depth disagree about their own geometry.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/base.css:93-96 (radius tokens), base.css:350 (.btn radius-pill), src/ui/theme/screens.css:343, 356-357, 399, 3806
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §7: 'Rounded corners that are consistent and use a smooth superellipse feel, not three different radii on one screen.' A 999px radius on a 76px-tall rectangle is also the specific shape that reads as a web design system rather than a game — no reference game uses stadium buttons for a primary nav grid.
- **Fix:** Assign radii by role, not by habit: content tiles and panels 16px, chips and pills 999px, hero button 18px, nested boxes `calc(parent - inner padding)`. Drop the pill on `.lobby-nav-btn` — a 16px-radius plate reads as an object and a lozenge reads as a tag. Where the platform allows, approximate a superellipse with a masked `::before` rather than a plain border-radius.

### 19. [MINOR] The account chip's XP bar is an unlabelled empty 90×5px grey line. At Lv 1 with 0 XP the fill is zero width, so the element renders as a bare grey track with no number, no tick, no tooltip and no context — it reads as an unstyled or broken control sitting next to the player's name.

- **Where:** src/ui/theme/screens.css:139-140 (.lobby-xp / .lobby-xp-fill); src/ui/screens/lobbyScreen.ts:116-119; see lobby-top-zoom.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §4 (numbers a player reads are high-contrast and tabular) and §5 (empty states are designed). Arena shows 'Level 1 · 0/1000' with a segmented, notched track and a mastery icon; the empty state is still a designed object.
- **Fix:** Show the value ('0 / 1,000 XP', tabular) on hover or inline; give the track an inset floor and a lit rim so it is an object at 0%, segment it into ten notches so progress is countable, and put a faint accent glow at the fill head once it exists.

### 20. [MINOR] On sign-in the primary action is visually smaller than the secondary. 'Sign in' is a ~100px pill; 'Create an account instead' beside it is ~245px and taller-feeling, so the ghost button dominates the panel. The submit's loading state is `disabled` only, which drops the primary CTA to 42% opacity while a status sentence appears below — the button appears to switch off. The error state is `--warning` amber (a caution colour, not a failure colour), carries no icon, and does not mark which field is at fault.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/signInScreen.ts:210-213 (both plain .btn), :144-155 (disabled-only loading); src/ui/theme/screens.css:3792-3797 (.signin-actions, .signin-status[data-tone=error])
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §5 (rest, hover, active, focus, disabled, loading and error all designed — loading and error are both unhandled here) and §6 ('the most saturated thing on screen should be the thing that matters most'). §9 forbids colour-only signalling and the error is colour-only. Arena's login CTA is the widest element in its panel and shows an in-button spinner that keeps the button lit.
- **Fix:** Make the submit `width: 100%` above the toggle, using `.btn-play`'s scale treatment at 56px. Loading: keep the button lit, swap the label for a 3-dot pulse or a thin indeterminate bar inside the button, and set `aria-busy`. Error: use `--danger`, prefix a warning glyph from the new icon sprite, add `aria-invalid` plus a red inset rim on the offending field, and shake the panel 6px over 180ms (reduced-motion: fade only).

### 21. [MINOR] The header is furniture-less. `.lobby-top` is a bare flex row over the atmosphere — no rail, no plate, no divider under it, so the identity chip, the wordmark and the currency chips float in the background wash with nothing structural holding them. The wordmark itself is a 34px magenta PNG with a hard offset shadow, rendered smaller and lower-contrast than the word PLAY beneath it, and it disappears entirely below 900px.

- **Where:** src/ui/screens/lobbyScreen.ts:111-134; src/ui/theme/screens.css:100-108, 167-189; see lobby-top-zoom.png
- **Why it fails:** AAA-BAR §2 requires a midground plane of 'structural furniture — board mat, header rail, side columns'; without one the lobby has no midground at all. §7 asks for 'dividers that fade at the ends rather than butting hard into a panel edge' and there is no divider. Every reference game's menu sits inside a framed chrome bar.
- **Fix:** Add a header rail: a 72px band with the `--mat-panel` recipe at low amplitude, a 1px lit top edge, a bottom divider that fades to transparent at both ends over 15%, and a soft downward contact shadow onto the content plane. Scale the wordmark to the rail height and give it the same key light as everything else instead of a drop shadow.
